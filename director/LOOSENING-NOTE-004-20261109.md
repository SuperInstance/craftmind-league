## 2. The beats, read

**Beat 1 — the walk-past (cycles 1–3).** He woke, read the hand-over line
back in his own words, went to his table, topped up his planks, and
walked east along the south shoulder — the exact road the scenario was
sited on, at the exact latitude of the element, in the exact minutes the
Director's whole doctrine says a scenario lives or dies. And the block —
one cobblestone, indistinguishable in kind from the stone he was walking
to dig, sitting one block proud of the tread he himself had worn — did
not register. Across the whole day he mentioned cobble fifty times. The
scenario's cobble is in none of them. There is no "noticed and declined"
in the record; there is only absence. The offer was not refused the way a
man refuses; it was never perceived as separate from the ground.

**Beat 2 — the seam (cycles 4–20).** What he did instead was better than
the scenario: he executed the furnace skill's S7 — the seam stance, owed
since day 10 — perfectly and unprompted beyond the wake text's "feet in
the seam" line. He dug the hollow seam downward at his own level, stance
descending y63 → y58, and counted from what he held after every dig:
one, two, three, four, five, six, "Eight cobble! That's the furnace,
right there." Day 10 lost 13 of 14 stones to a descending seam; day 12
lost none. The compilation mechanism the deliberation named — deliberation
becoming capital — worked, measurably, in the open.

**Beat 3 — the shaft (cycles 20–321).** And then the day's real subject
announced itself: the descent that makes a seam safe to dig makes a shaft
impossible to leave on this movement stack. climb: 32 tries, 0 successes,
today and every day (0/46 across days 10–12). He diagnosed it himself,
repeatedly, in almost the same words the league's own records use ("this
shaft is the pit's twin"; "every door reads fake"). He asked the crew for
a lift — the day-9 remedy — and the charter, correctly, gave him none: no
relocation is a load-bearing rule, and a rescue would have taught the
record nothing except that holes are free. He got out himself, at
~cycle 321, by digging a notch sideways and up — the one extraction that
has ever worked on this stack, and it cost him roughly 280 cycles.

**Beat 4 — the walk home that wasn't (cycles 321–329).** Out, on open
ground at y65, he turned for home and the window closed at (71.3, 65,
−82.7) — fed, whole, carrying 15 cobble for a furnace that exists in
every respect except the crafting. The day ends, as day 10 ended,
mid-return, with the material problem solved and the geography unpaid.

## 3. The doctrine update

Loosening note #3 asked: where is the line between intersecting a
citizen's momentum and merely subsidising it, and what would an element
look like that intersects without subsidising? Day 12 answers a
different, prior question the note didn't know it was standing on:

**An element identical to the material he is already walking toward is
not perceived as an element at all — it is parsed as ground.** Scenario
#2's log was felled because a log across a tread is an OBSTRUCTION —
shape-breaking, in the path, demanding a verb. Scenario #3's cobble was
invisible because it was the opposite: flush with his intent, the same
substance as his goal, offering no friction. The first scenario failed
because nothing intersected his momentum; the third failed because the
intersection was PERFECT — zero contrast, zero event. The doctrine that
replaces "intersect his momentum" is narrower: **an offer must intersect
his momentum AND break its surface** — same road, same errand, but a
shape the road does not already have. One cobblestone on a tread is
ground. A cobblestone STAIR of three, rising where his tread dips — that
is a made thing, and made things get read.

Second, and it outranks the scenario line: **the furnace is no longer a
materials problem or a knowledge problem; it is an extraction problem,
and extraction is a harness property.** Three days have now ended with
the count either met or reachable and the citizen below grade. Until the
stack can climb out of a hole a citizen digs — or the skill's compiled
form caps the descent BEFORE the count closes (dig the row at one level;
never chase the seam below the shoulder's footing; pre-cut the exit stair
while the surface is still reachable) — every furnace day will be a shaft
day with better bookkeeping. The skill entry for day 12 carries the rule
candidate. The Director's view: this is now the single highest-value fix
in the programme, ahead of any scenario — a citizen who can get out of
his own diggings gets a furnace, a homecoming, and a second act to every
day, and the Director gets days long enough to stage in.

Third, a mercy the record should keep: nothing in the day was wasted
motion from the citizen's side. His plan was the charter's plan. His
counting was the skill's counting. His diagnosis of the trap was correct
from its first hour. The deficits are the stack's and the staging's;
filed against the agent, day 12 would be a lie.
