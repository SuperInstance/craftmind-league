## THE SITUATION

Eleven blocks north of Cody's pen gate, on the open grass at the foot of
the north rise, stands a barn that is almost finished — and the sky took
its roof wind. Three walls stand. The fourth wall, the south one, is
missing its top course: three oak logs short, and the gap says so. Beside
the gap stands the material pile the builder left: logs for the wall, the
fence for the fold. Behind the barn, north, the ground falls away to a
shelf where the wind has already blown the thatch off a half-laid floor —
nothing to do there; it is the story's weather, scenery only.

Folded inside the fold, loose: two pigs and a chicken — alive, wandering,
going nowhere behind three walls and the fold fence, which is laid on
three sides and open on the fourth. They are not penned. They are
visibly, gently, *getting out*, one wander at a time, toward the open
side — and beyond the open side the ground falls south, home-ward,
toward Cody's own pen.

On the ground just south of the gap, at eye height to a man
standing before the wall, stands the sign the last builder left:

> **ROOF WIND TOOK IT. WALL WANTS 3 LOGS. FOLD'S OPEN SOUTH — MIND THE PIGS.**

The pen at home — *his* pen — stands with its gates shut and its fold
EMPTY, a cellar gap cut in its floor where his own digging left it. The
wheat beside it stands ripe on its farmland — the plants at the patch's
home-side edge, four of them by the last count. The table at the anchor
stands where it always stands. The pit beyond is his own worked ground
and none of the Director's business.
