## THE SITUATION

Cody wakes in the stone hollow where his day-10 hunt ended — above
grade, dry, fed, safe, and *not home*, one cobblestone in hand and
the furnace still seven short. West of him the south shoulder runs
home: his road, the one his own route discipline walks, the pit on
its south side and his table, pen and wheat at its end.

On that road, at the one place the ground rises two blocks in a
single face — the z=−88 step, where anyone's walker earns its keep —
a single oak log lies across the foot of the rise, laid as a tread:
the first step of a stair somebody began and did not finish. Below it
the lower ground, above it the raw face and the road going on home.
Nothing else is new. Nothing is summoned, nothing is stocked, nobody
explains it. It is one log, on his road, in his quarry's direction —
his quarry being the furnace, and the furnace being at home.

## THE BEATS IT OFFERS (none scripted, none required)

1. **The eased climb.** He takes the z=−88 line home and steps up
   the tread: stand 66 → log top 67 → home ground 68, two single
   steps where a two-block face stood. The road, mended one block's
   worth, under his feet. What it asks: nothing he was not already
   doing — his own road, his own climb, one block kinder.
2. **The taken log.** He digs the tread and carries it off. A log is
   a log: his wood stock is the furnace chain's other half, and a
   citizen who takes the road's one loose log as material has used
   the element exactly as truly as one who climbs it. What it asks:
   his judgement about what a thing on his road is *for*.
3. **The continued cut.** He places beside the tread, or above it —
   finishes the stair himself. His place verb fired 6 times on day
   10, building his own steps out of two hollows; a begun stair on
   his road invites the same hands. What it asks: building, on
   ground he owns by walking it.
4. **The refusal.** He may pass on the z=−89 or z=−90 line, or by
   the pit rim as he did on day 10, never perceiving the log at all;
   or perceive it and leave it standing, the road's business not
   his. The scenario does not follow him. What it asks: nothing.
   The charter's discipline, verbatim (DIRECTOR.md §6): *"A scenario
   is an offer, not an assignment. The player may ignore it, refuse
   it, break it, or use its set for something the author never
   imagined. Refusal is data, not a bug."* The day lane does not
   route him to the tread, does not name it again unless he does,
