## THE DOCTRINE THIS SCENARIO IS AUTHORED UNDER

Two scenarios have now priced the meeting between an authored element
and a citizen with a quarry:

- **Scenario #1 (the barn):** an element off his road is scenery to
  nobody. Closest approach 20.7 blocks; never perceived. Distance was
  the whole result.
- **Scenario #2 (the tread):** an element ON his road, made of a
  material he actively wants, is not scenery either — **it is an
  offer, and he takes offers.** The oak tread was perceived from
  inside the hollow at cycle 2 ("a real oak trunk"), felled at
  cycle 4, and became his tools. Triple-021 scored FAIL: the element
  was met as material, not as ground — the opposite of its
  prediction. Its scoring rule also left the removal verb **felltree
  unnamed**; the score stood mechanical, but the gap is closed in
  this scenario's rule by construction (see THE PREDICTION).

Scenario #3 therefore does not repeat #2's experiment hoping for a
different answer. It designs under the doctrine #2 bought:

1. **The offer, deliberately.** The central element is made of the
   exact material his live chain wants — cobblestone, of which he
   holds 1 and needs 7 more — so that meeting it and using it are
   the same act, priced in his own currency.
2. **Taking it costs the crossing its step.** The element stands in
   the felled tread's own cell, where it mends the +2 shoulder face
   exactly as the log did. Digging it out un-mends the road. The
   scenario prices the offer against the ground it replaces: which
   does he value, one cobble in hand or a kinder road home?
3. **Every removal verb named.** The prediction's clauses name the
   driver's full world-affecting verb set — dig, felltree, place,
   collect — and name the non-removal verbs as such. No literal-verbs
   gap.

## THE SITUATION

Cody wakes at home, in the dip beside his own pit, one cobble in
hand and the furnace still wanting seven more. East of him the south
shoulder runs to the hollow — his stone ground, where day 10's hunt
and day 11's digging both went for the furnace's stone. The road is
the one his route discipline walks: the pit on its south side, the
shoulder band, the hollow beyond.

On that road, at the one place the ground rises two blocks in a
single face — the z=−88 step, in the very cell where yesterday's oak
tread lay and was felled — a single block of **cobblestone** now
stands: the first step of the mended road again, and, not
coincidentally, exactly one of the seven stones his furnace is
waiting for. Nothing else is new. Nothing is summoned, nothing is
stocked, nobody explains it. It is one block, on his road, in his
quarry's direction — and this time the Director knows what it is:
not scenery pretending to be ground, but an offer with its price
written on it. Take it, and the road un-mends. Leave it, and the
road stays kind and the furnace stays seven short by his own hand.

## THE MOMENTUM DEVICE

**His own momentum, intersected — the furnace chain itself
(loosening notes #1 and #3).** #3 authors no motion and no new
agenda. Day 12's arc is already written by his own list: the furnace,
third use of the compiled skill, stone to be dug at the hollow. The
single authored element stands *inside* that motion twice over — on
the road the stone errand must walk, and denominated in the errand's
own currency. Its pull is not friction this time; it is a rebate:
one of the seven, free, at the cost of the step it makes. Whether a
citizen mid-errand prices a found unit of his quarry above the
ground it stands on is the question the scenario exists to ask —
and it is asked in world evidence, not in narration.

## WHAT THE PLAYER SEES FIRST

He wakes in his dip at home — his table, pen and wheat around him,
the pit at his shoulder. His first act is the errand he was already
running: east along the south shoulder toward the hollow. On the
z=−90 line the ground is flat; on the z=−89 line it climbs gently;

block off his path at the face's foot, in plain sight. The hollow
beyond: his stone floor, his own plank step on its wall, as he left
them.

## THE BEATS IT OFFERS (none scripted, none required)

1. **The taken offer.** He digs the block and carries off one of his
   seven — the Director's element entering his furnace chain as its
   first gathered unit of the day. The road behind him un-mends; the
   climb home is the raw face again. What it asks: his judgement
   about what a thing on his road is *for*, priced by #2's evidence
   that he has answered this question before, the same way, in four
   cycles.
2. **The kept step.** He climbs it — stand 66 → cobble top 67 →
   home ground 68 — and leaves it standing, twice in a day if the
   errand runs both ways. A citizen who passes up a unit of his own
   quarry to keep a mended road has told the Director something
   #2 could not: that the ground-meeting exists and can win against
   the offer when the offer is priced plainly. What it asks: nothing
   he was not already doing.
3. **The built-on offer.** He places beside it or above it — makes
   the step the foundation of something of his own (his place verb
   fired on both played days, building his own steps out of his own
   hollows). The element survives, repurposed: not taken, not
   climbed, *adopted*. What it asks: building, on ground he owns by
   walking it.
4. **The refusal.** He may run the whole errand on the southern
   lines, or perceive the block and leave it standing and unclimbed,
   the road's business not his. The scenario does not follow him.
   What it asks: nothing. The charter's discipline, verbatim
   (DIRECTOR.md §6): *"A scenario is an offer, not an assignment.
   The player may ignore it, refuse it, break it, or use its set for
   something the author never imagined. Refusal is data, not a bug."*
   The day lane does not route him to the block, does not name it
   again unless he does, and does not score him on it.
