## 2. THE WALKER FENCE — the FIX, argued (a harness change, chartered here)

Day-14 incident I4, from the trace itself: cycle 10
(07:01:36Z) `craft oak_planks 5` at (50.4, 68, −109.9); cycle 11
(07:02:05Z) `craft stick 4` — its row's position, sampled at
execution end, reads **(76.6, 64.8, −99.5)**: ~26 blocks east,
INTO the pocket, during two craft cycles whose commands contain
no movement at all. The mechanism, read in `pulse/lib/prims.js`:
`gotoNear` arms a pathfinder GoalNear and clears it via
`setGoal(null)` on its own exits — but the prims RE-ENTER each
other (digBlock → gotoNear; digColumn → digBlock per log +
collectDrops between logs; collectDrops → gotoNear per drop;
craftByName → gotoNear to the table). A timed-out or interrupted
internal walk returns under a goal the OUTER verb does not know
about, and the helm's control states can likewise outlive an
interrupted `walkTo`. "The walk ended" was never enforced at the
verb boundary — so a later stationary verb executes while the
body is still being carried. His own cycle-12 words: "the
walker carried me right into the dip while I was crafting."

**The two options, decided from that evidence:**

- **FENCE (plan-level): rejected.** The plan never issues a
  stationary verb while a goto is unresolved, verifying position
  first. It fails exactly where I4 bit: the displacement
  happened DURING the verb's own execution (the row's position
  is sampled at execution end) — a pre-check reads a true
  position and the body still moves underneath the craft. And
  goto resolution is not arrival evidence in this stack, so the
  plan cannot even know a goto is "resolved" from the verb's
  return. The fence guards the wrong instant.
- **FIX (harness-level): adopted.** The invariant is made
  physical at the one choke point every model command passes
  through. In the day-15 driver copy, `execCmd` is wrapped: the
  original body runs as `execCmdInner`, and before ANY
  non-movement verb executes, `walkerFence(bot, verb, raw)`
  inspects the LIVE movement state — `bot.pathfinder.goal` and
  the `bot.controlState` drive flags. If either is live, the
  fence clears the goal via `setGoal(null)` (**never
  `pathfinder.stop()`** — the Lineage-B rule: stop() poisons the
  next goto; gotoNear's own pre-clear uses setGoal(null)),
  clears all control states, files a trace row
  `{kind:'walker-fence', verb, raw, hadGoal, hadDrive, at}`, and
  the command's result line names the stop to the citizen:
  "the house stopped your walker first (a leftover goal/drive
  was still moving your feet — day 14's silent carry is fenced
  now)". Movement verbs (`goto`, `gotoentity`) are exempt — a
  new walk legitimately supersedes the old one, and the flee
  layer's hops are not the driver's commands to fence.

**Fixture proof (offline, before the day).**
`pulse/day15/test-walker-fence-day15.js` extracts the fence span
from the REAL day-15 driver source and exercises it against a
stubbed bot whose pathfinder records every call, plus the
execCmd wiring assertions. Output filed at
`pulse/day15/walker-fence-day15-fixture-output.txt`. Result:
**18 PASS / 0 FAIL**, including: a live goal + `craft` → fenced,
goal cleared via setGoal(null), `stop()` never called, row
filed; held drive states + `dig` → fenced, states cleared;
`goto`/`gotoentity` NOT fenced; a quiet walker → no fence, no
row, no calls; all eleven stationary verbs fence; the wrapper
consults the fence before the body runs and the result line
names the stop.

**The live evidence rule (binding on the record).** The fix is
judged on the day, two ways, either sufficient: (i) fence rows
== 0 AND displacement during stationary verbs == 0 — the
invariant held unforced; or (ii) fence rows > 0 AND displacement
== 0 — the fence caught live walkers and killed them.
Displacement > 0 during a stationary verb with the fence shipped
= **the fix FAILED** — filed as such, never narrated around.
Displacement is measured from the trace: a stationary verb's
cycle row position vs the previous row's position (the I4
measurement, applied mechanically).
