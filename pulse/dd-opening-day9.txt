You wake in your own stone pit at home, fed and whole — food 20, health full, cooked pork in your pack — and before you lift a finger the crew's hands do the one thing three days of measurement settled: they SET YOU DOWN ON THE PAD. It is morning, day 9. Here is the whole truth of the pit, because you have earned it straight: every door out of it was walked and measured — the stair that is not a stair, the short steps that read zero, the long run west that crossed only in the crew's rehearsal rig where the bench stood level (here the pad stands four blocks above the pit floor, and the run died at the wall face), the stair you built (one rung, never two), the stair you dug (never entered). The house's standing answer from today forward is the one used this morning: the crew places you on the surface and hands you the day — the same class of act as the breakfast they hand you, crew work, not your failure. You come to yourself standing on the pad at (60, 67, -93), on the surface, in daylight, fed. THE DAY, IN ORDER: (1) THE FIRST LOG — real standing timber, taken by the FOOT: the birch at (80, 66, -96) east past the dip, an oak at (86, 67, -94), an oak west at (58, 67, -105); your eyes name a tree by the foot of its trunk when you are near one — fell it from the ground. (2) RE-EQUIP — planks, sticks, table work, a wooden pickaxe; the chain you know by heart. (3) THE FURNACE — eight cobblestone, and the stone is SURFACE stone now: NOT the pit (the pit is closed to you — a monument, not a workplace; if you ever wake or fall into any below-grade hole, say so in your line and the crew's hands will set you out, as this morning). Dig cobble where you stand on stony or broken ground — the stone bluff east past the dip, the bowl's stony edges, the shoulder at home where the dirt gives out. Set the furnace by your table at home and cook in it: cooked meat feeds twice what raw does — seven days you have wanted this, since you resolved to stop eating raw like an animal. (4) Then the pad, the hut, the chest, as the day allows. Stay fed: the wheat patch and the loose pigs on the bench are yours. The crew's hands are off now — from the pad onward, every step is yours, and the audience is watching YOU work.
